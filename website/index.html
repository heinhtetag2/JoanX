Why it works

A safety app your child won't want to switch off

Blockers get turned off. Schedules get learned. Trackers get resented. JoanX takes the other side — it gives your child something to lose by ignoring it.

One buzz, never a block

A single vibration, then a card, then their buddy asking them to look up. The phone is never taken away — and calls and texts can never be blocked.

Looking up is how you win

Every phone-free minute pays points. Points hatch eggs, grow a buddy through ten levels, and unlock the ten villains. Stop fastest and you earn the most.

Progress, not surveillance

You get a weekly read on whether the behaviour is changing. No map, no location trail, no reading their messages — and your child is shown that list by name.

Getting started

Set up in about three minutes

STEP 1

Pair their phone

Install JoanX on your child's phone and scan the code your app shows. No wearable, no new hardware, nothing to buy.

STEP 2

They walk, it watches one thing

Only while walking, and only for walking plus phone use at the same time. The rest of the day, JoanX does nothing at all.

STEP 3

You read the week

How often they stopped when warned, how fast, and how much less this is happening than in their first week.

The safety moment

Ten seconds of grace, then one buzz

A child already putting their phone away is never warned. If they keep going, JoanX escalates in tone — gentle, then firm, then urgent — and never in force.

10s grace, nothing happens 1 vibration, never repeated 2s to stop before any screen shows Card one line, one button 5s quiet, then firmer

The best outcome has no screen at all

Look up within two seconds of the buzz and no warning is ever drawn — and that path pays the most points.

Tapping "got it" is not enough

Only actually stopping pays. Dismissing the card earns nothing, and the app quietly re-checks five seconds later.

It gets firmer. It never gets mean

The strongest thing JoanX can do is say so plainly — and note it in your weekly report.

For your child

A game that only safe walking can pay for

Ten points a minute, phone down. Fifty for catching themselves before the warning. One currency, earned one way — so every price in the shop is an amount of walking, and nothing can be bought with your card.

A buddy that visibly grows

Ten levels, three stages. It transforms the moment the level lands — even mid-battle.

The rarest buddies are earned, not bought

A seven-day streak hands one over. Thirty days hands over an Epic. A child who never spends a point can still collect the best of them.

Rooms, friends, no chat

Visit a friend's room, leave a like or one screened line. There is no messaging between children, by design.

The villains

Ten habits with names, fought one at a time

Every villain on the road is something that really pulls a child's eyes down. Ping is the notification that never stops asking. Chrono is the hour that disappears inside the screen. Tapping one opens its card — what it does, and what beating it pays.

No skipping ahead

Each win unlocks the next villain, so how far along the road they are is a record of how long they have been walking safely.

A character, not a stat block

Each one has a story and a move of its own, written to put a name to a habit rather than to look frightening.

Five tries a day

Battles are capped. This is a reward for a day of safe walking, not something to sit and grind.

The battle

Fought with a buddy their walking built

The buddy stepping into the ring is the one they grew. Its level, its stats and its odds all trace back to minutes spent with the phone down — so the only way to get stronger is to keep looking up.

The odds are shown first

Win chance, both power totals and a recommended level, before they commit — so a loss is never a nasty surprise.

A win pays twice

Points towards the next egg and XP towards the next level — and a buddy can evolve right there, mid-fight.

It stops when they start walking

Begin moving and the battle closes itself. The game is never allowed to compete with the road.

For you

One page a week, and it answers the real question

Not how many times it happened — how much less it is happening than in your child's own first week. That is the number that tells you it is working.

Four numbers, each against last week

Warnings that ended in a stop, safe-walking minutes, seconds to look up, and the current streak.

A summary in plain language

The verdict first, then the week's story, then one thing to try — not a wall of charts at 11pm.

A hard knock reaches you

If a fall is detected, the phone asks your child if they are okay for twenty seconds. No answer, and you are told right away.

One dashboard, every child

Up to five children on one account, each with their own rules, device and week. You switch between them at the top of the page.

Settings and alerts

Told when something happened, not a feed to watch

Each child has their own page — the phone paired to them, what they agreed to at setup, and whether the game is on. And when something real happens, you hear about it once.

You can see what they agreed to

Motion, screen use, notifications: exactly which protections your child accepted at setup, and which they turned down.

Two guardians, one alert

Once one of you has checked a moment, the other sees that it is handled — so nobody chases the same thing twice.

It says when protection drops

FAQ

Questions parents ask first

Does it lock or block my child's phone?

No. JoanX never blocks the screen. The strongest thing it does is show a firmer message from your child's buddy and note the moment in your weekly report. Calls and texts can never be blocked at all, so your child can always reach you.

Can I see where my child is?

No, and that is deliberate. JoanX collects no location data. It uses the phone's motion sensor to notice walking, and nothing else. A child who feels tracked uninstalls the app, and then it protects nobody.

How does it know they are walking and using the phone?

The phone's own accelerometer. Walking is confirmed from a steady step cadence held for about eight seconds; if the screen is in use at the same time for ten seconds or more, the safety moment begins. Everything happens on the device.

Does my child need a smartwatch or any other device?

No. JoanX runs on the phone they already carry — nothing to buy, nothing to charge.

Can my child just turn it off?

Their phone pairs to your account rather than to a person, so a child cannot quietly unlink themselves — only a guardian can. They can dismiss a warning card, but dismissing earns them nothing, and JoanX re-checks moments later.

Can children message each other?

There is no chat in JoanX. Children can visit each other's rooms, leave a like, and write a one-line guestbook note that is screened before it posts. That is the whole social layer.

How many children can I add?

Up to five, each with their own rules, their own buddy and their own weekly report. You switch between them at the top of the dashboard, and other adults who help — a co-parent, a grandparent, the school-run circle — can be added to a group for any of them.
