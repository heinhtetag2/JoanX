JoanX: getting children to look up while they walk

A walking-safety game for children and a calm weekly dashboard for their parents. It answers phone-use-while-walking with one buzz instead of a locked screen, and makes stopping the thing that wins the game.

02 · Context

Who uses it, and where

Two people use JoanX, in two very different moments.

THE CHILD · ON THE WALK

A school-age child with their own phone

  • Walks to school or around town with the phone in hand
  • Glances at notifications, short videos, feeds
  • Needs: to keep using the phone, and to not feel watched
THE PARENT · LATER, AT HOME

A guardian who wants reassurance

  • Isn't there during the walk; checks in afterwards
  • Decides whether to talk to the child about phone habits
  • Needs: to know if it's getting better, without spying
OTHER GUARDIANS

Co-parents, grandparents, the school run

  • Share care of the same child
  • Join a guardian group by QR or code, approved by an admin
  • Needs: one shared picture, no duplicate alarms

03 · The core problem

Change the behaviour without taking the phone away

How might we get a child to look up while walking, when every tool that forces it gets switched off?

DESIGN RISK 1
The child wants to
keep watching or replying while walking
What gets in the way
a blocker locks the screen, so they switch it off or borrow a friend's phone
Why it matters
a safety app that gets turned off protects nobody
DESIGN RISK 2
The parent wants to
know their child is safer
What gets in the way
trackers answer with a map and a feed of every event
Why it matters
a child who feels watched uninstalls, and the parent loses even the little they had
DESIGN RISK 3
The child needs to
stop in the moment, not later
What gets in the way
a warning that's loud or constant becomes noise and gets dismissed
Why it matters
the moment passes in seconds; the nudge has to land first time

These are design risks, not observed pain points. They come from the product brief and spec, not from research in this repo. If you interviewed parents or watched children use phones, replace them with what you saw, e.g. "7 of 8 parents said…".

04 · Goals and constraints

What a better experience had to do

Goals

For the child

  • Stop quickly, without losing the phone
  • Get something they care about for stopping

For the parent

  • See whether it's working, in one look
  • Hear about real problems only

For the product

  • Stay installed: be something a child doesn't want to switch off

Constraints that shaped the work

  • Never block the screen. The strongest action is a firmer message.
  • Calls and texts always stay open (spec F-27).
  • No location. The danger-zone and GPS features were cut from scope in the 18 Jun spec revision.
  • Motion sensors only: walking is detected from step cadence, held for about 8 seconds.
  • Android first (foreground service, F-27).
  • A 43-row functional spec to trace every screen to.
  • Two languages: English and Korean, with 1,200+ Korean strings.

06 · Design approach

Four principles behind every choice

  1. Nudge, never forceEscalate the tone, never the power. The best outcome has no screen at all.
  2. Reward the right momentPay for stopping, not for dismissing. Stopping early pays the most.
  3. Show change, not surveillanceParents see whether it's improving, never where the child is.
  4. Same truth for both sidesWhat the parent can see is a short, fixed list, and the child can be shown it.
07 · Three problem-solving stories

Where the hard decisions were

1

The safety moment

Interrupting a child mid-walk without making them switch the app off.

Challenge
A warning has seconds to work. Too soft and it's ignored; too hard and the app gets uninstalled. It also can't block anything.
Exploration
Seven warning layouts were built and compared side by side, plus two narrators:
SpotlightCardFrameSignalStripSheetBanner
King Cubix narratesThe child's buddy narrates
Decision
A staged sequence: 10 seconds of grace → one buzz → a one-line card → a firmer message, only if they keep going. King Cubix, a separate mascot, delivers the warning, so the child's own buddy isn't the one scolding them.
UX improvement
A child who looks up within 2 seconds of the buzz never sees a warning at all, and that path pays the most points. Stopping becomes the quickest way through.
UI improvement
  • One line and one button per step
  • The button was renamed to "Okay, got it", and its check icon was dropped so it doesn't read as a reward
  • A three-tier tone ladder: gentle → firm → urgent
  • From the second reminder, a small note: "recorded in your report"
Trade-off
No lockout, even after repeated ignoring. A determined child can keep walking head-down; JoanX records it instead of stopping it.
  1. The buzz marker: no card yet, so the child can stop before any screen appears
  2. The card: one line, one button, King Cubix with a hand raised
  3. The firmer line, shown only if they keep walking head-down
2

A game only safe walking can pay for

Making stopping more rewarding than scrolling, without turning it into a slot machine.

Challenge
The reward has to matter to a child, but a game aimed at children can easily become a gacha that pressures them to spend.
Exploration
Several character lines (comic, Korean-style, 3D, 3D-cute), 20 collection layouts, 10 dex layouts, and several battle presentations before the painted versus arena.
ComicKorean3D3D CuteClient line
Decision
  • One currency, points, earned by safe walking: 10 per phone-down minute, 50 for stopping before a warning
  • Villains are real habits (Ping is notifications, Chrono is lost time), unlocked one win at a time
  • Epic buddies can't be bought; they come from a 30-day streak, level 10 or the final villain
UX improvement
Every reward traces back to walking safely, so the game reinforces the behaviour instead of competing with it. Battles close themselves when the child starts walking.
UI improvement
  • Win chance and both power totals are shown before a battle, so a loss isn't a surprise
  • The result screen shows the battle maths
  • Gold is reserved for points and XP only, so value always reads the same way
Trade-off
No real-money purchases and no chat between children: only room visits, likes and a screened one-line note. That means less "engagement", on purpose.
  1. The villain road: ten habits, unlocked in order
  2. A villain card: the habit it stands for, its move, and the reward
  3. The result: points and XP, with the battle maths shown
3

A weekly report, not a live feed

Giving parents reassurance without turning JoanX into surveillance.

Challenge
Parents want to know it's working, but a stream of events and a map would erode the child's trust, and the whole approach depends on that trust.
Exploration
The report's key numbers were redesigned several times in July and August: two-card KPIs, then streak swapped for an "Ignored" card, then a ratio "stopped" KPI, which was reverted to a plain count. Two KPI styles were kept to compare.
CardsRing + stats
Decision
  • Four numbers against last week: stopped when warned, ignored, safe-walking minutes, streak
  • An AI summary that starts with the verdict, then the week's story, then one thing to try
  • Alerts only for real events: a possible fall, a device offline, protection switched off
UX improvement
The first thing a parent reads answers "is it getting better?". Two guardians don't chase the same alert: once one checks it, the other sees "already checked".
UI improvement
  • Verdict-first hierarchy
  • "Warning acceptance" renamed to the plainer "Stopped when warned"
  • Urgent alerts marked in red with a call button
  • Offline and permission states shown instead of failing silently
Trade-off
No location, no app names, no messages. Parents get less detail than a tracker offers, in exchange for an app the child keeps.
  1. This week: four numbers, each against last week
  2. Alerts: urgent first, with "already checked" for shared guardians
  3. Groups: everyone who helps sees the same week

08 · The complete experience

How the pieces fit together

  1. PairParent shows a 6-digit code; the child types it in
  2. ConsentThe child accepts motion, usage, overlay and notification permissions
  3. WalkDetection starts only while walking and using the phone
  4. NudgeBuzz, card, message
  5. PlayPoints grow the buddy and unlock villains
  6. ReportThe parent reads the week

Also designed: code expiry, device offline, permission switched off, parent empty states, a 20-second fall check, and new-device approval. See them all in the prototype.

What was checked, and what would be tested first

NOT YET DONE

Task: "Walk to the corner while watching a video." Observe what the child does at the buzz.

Look for: do they stop before the card appears? Do they read the card, or tap it away?

Parent task: "Is your child walking more safely this week?" Can they answer from the report alone?
